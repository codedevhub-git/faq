01 What Kind of Website Do I Get?

Static Website vs Web Application — What's The Difference?

A static website is like a digital brochure. The pages look the same for everyone and nothing changes unless someone manually edits the code. A web application is alive — it thinks, reacts, and changes based on what YOU and your customers do.

📄 Static Website
  • Same content for everyone
  • Can't send you notifications
  • Can't track or filter data
  • Can't grow with your business
  • Can be hosted anywhere (GoDaddy, Wix, etc.)
⚡ Web Application
  • Reacts to users in real time
  • Sends you texts/emails when leads come in
  • Filters, searches, and organizes data
  • Grows with your business — add tools later
  • Needs a server (like a brain to run on)
Example Scenarios

💈 A Barbershop — Static Website

A barbershop just needs to show up online. Services, prices, hours, location, and a few photos. Nothing fancy — just a clean, professional site that makes them look legit when someone Googles them.

This is a static website. It looks great, it ranks on Google, but it doesn't DO anything on its own. No notifications, no booking, no data. Just a beautiful digital presence.

🚗 An Auto Detailing Business — Web Application

An auto detailing shop has a beautiful site but every time someone wants to book, they have to call or send an email. The owner misses calls constantly because he's in the garage working on cars.

Now with a web application: Customer fills out a booking form on the site → Owner gets a TEXT instantly → The booking is saved automatically → Owner can see all upcoming jobs in one place. Simple. Not too complex. But it actually works FOR him.

A landscaping company has 5 employees, trucks, equipment, and materials they buy every week. They were tracking everything on paper and in group texts. Employees didn't know their schedule until the morning of. Materials got lost or double-bought.

A simple website is like painting a wall. A web application is like building the entire house. More moving parts, more skill, more time. But the ROI is completely different.

📄
Static Website

Just pages and images. No backend. No database. No logic. Quick to build, quick to host anywhere.

Web Application

Needs a backend (the brain). Needs a database (the memory). Needs logic (the thinking). Needs integrations (SMS, email, etc.). Needs a real server to run on. More work = more cost. But it actually DOES something for your business.

03 CMS — Can I Update My Site Myself?

What Is A CMS?

CMS stands for Content Management System. Think of it as a control panel for your website — like the dashboard in your car. You don't need to know how the engine works. You just press buttons and things happen.

Real Life Example

🏗️ Construction Company — David

David just finished a big project and wants to add 15 new photos to his website. Without a CMS, he has to call his developer every single time. With a CMS, he logs into a simple dashboard, uploads the photos, writes a short description, clicks 'Publish' — done. No coding. No phone calls. No waiting.

💰
How Much Does CMS Cost?

CMS is an extra layer that has to be custom built for your specific site. It's not just a button I flip on. It adds to the build price depending on how much control you want. Want to update just photos? That's simpler. Want full control over text, pages, images, and layout? That's more complex. We'll go over the exact cost based on what YOU need.

📸
What About Bulk Photos?

If you need to upload a lot of photos at once (like 50+ project photos), I charge by bulk. It's way cheaper than paying per photo. We'll set that up based on how many images you typically need to add at a time.

How Do I Know If I Need A CMS?

Ask yourself these questions:

Do I finish new projects regularly and want to show them on my site?
☐

05 SEO — Will Google Find My Site?

What Is SEO And Why Does It Matter?

SEO = Search Engine Optimization. It's how Google decides whether your website shows up when someone searches for what you do. Most people think SEO is just about keywords. It's way more than that.

1

Page Structure

Google reads your page like a book. It looks at headings (H1, H2, H3) to understand what your page is about. If your headings are messy or missing, Google gets confused and ranks you lower.

Real Life Example

A fence company in Charlotte has a page called 'Services'. If the H1 says 'Custom Fence Installation in Charlotte NC' and the H2s break down each service type — Google understands exactly what that page is about and shows it to people searching for fences in Charlotte.

2

Breadcrumbs

Breadcrumbs are the little navigation trail you see at the top of pages like 'Home > Services > Pool Installation'. They tell Google exactly where each page sits in your website. It helps Google understand your site structure and ranks you better.

Real Life Example

A landscaping company has 50 projects. Each project has its own page. Breadcrumbs: Home > Projects > Backyard Gardens > Project #23. Google now knows this page is about a specific garden project. It can show it to someone searching for 'backyard garden landscaping near me'.

3

How AI Search Reads Your Site

Google now uses AI to understand pages. ChatGPT and other AI tools also pull information from websites. They don't just look at keywords — they read your content and understand the MEANING behind it. If your page clearly explains what you do, who you serve, and where you are, AI search tools will recommend you.

Real Life Example

Someone asks ChatGPT: 'What are the best pool builders in California?' If your site clearly states you build pools in California, has detailed project pages, and good reviews — there's a real chance AI tools will mention you. This is the NEW way people find businesses.

4

Page Speed

Google HATES slow websites. If your page takes more than 3 seconds to load, people leave and Google ranks you lower. Especially on mobile — most people browse on their phones.

Real Life Example

A pool company has 50 photos on their homepage — all uploaded at full size. The page takes 8 seconds to load on mobile. Google sees this and pushes them down in search results. Another pool company optimizes their images (smaller file size, same quality) and loads in 1.5 seconds. Google ranks them higher.

5

Mobile First

Google crawls (reads) your mobile version FIRST now. If your site looks bad on a phone, Google doesn't care how good it looks on a desktop. Mobile is priority #1.

6

Meta Tags & Descriptions

Meta descriptions are the short text you see under each result in Google. They don't directly affect ranking but they make people CLICK on your result. A good meta description = more clicks = Google thinks your page is valuable = higher ranking.

Real Life Example

Bad meta: 'Preheim Pools - Home'. Good meta: 'Custom pool builders in California. 50+ completed projects, free quotes, full landscaping included. Contact us today.' The second one makes you WANT to click.

7

Structured Data (Schema)

This is code that tells Google exactly what your business IS. Your name, address, phone, services, reviews — all in a format Google understands instantly. It helps you show up in Google's 'knowledge panels' and rich results.

All Of This Is Built Into Every Site I Create

Keywords are just one piece. Real SEO is about structure, speed, mobile optimization, and helping Google (and AI) understand your business. That's what separates sites that get found from sites that don't.
